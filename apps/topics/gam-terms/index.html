Generalized Additive Models: Fitting Four Kinds of Term

log price = β0 + f1(age) + β2·size + γ(type) + f4(east, north) + ε

Simulated house prices with one term of each kind: a smooth curve in age, a straight line in size, one effect per house type, and a smooth surface over location (an interaction between the two map coordinates). Every panel shows the true term (dotted, or hollow diamonds) and the GAM's estimate (solid, or filled circles). Change n and the df of the two smooth terms and watch the curve and the map go from flat to noisy. Then use the fitting slider to replay backfitting one update at a time. Each update re-fits one term to the partial residual, the data with the other three current terms removed (the grey points). Older houses sit near the pricey north-west neighbourhood, so in the first sweep the age curve soaks up some of the location effect, and the terms then trade it back. The age curve and the map are penalized splines (P-splines), the version of the smoothing spline that pygam and mgcv use. The map is a tensor-product spline. (ISL 7.7, ESL 9.1.)

Keyboard and screen reader users: all controls are sliders and buttons (Shift+arrow moves a slider 5 steps). Each chart has a text description that updates with it. The tables at the bottom of the page hold every term's error, the house-type effects and the size slope, and they are announced when they change.

The four terms

  • true term (dotted)
  • GAM estimate (solid)
  • partial residual for that term (grey points)
  • true type effect (hollow diamond)
  • estimated type effect (filled circle)

Every term is centered to mean zero over the houses, so only its shape matters. The highlighted panel title is the term changed by the current update. Bottom right: each house's fitted log price against its true mean log price; a perfect fit lies on the dashed 45° line.

The location term f4(east, north)

  • blue = cheaper, red = pricier (scale bar under each map)
  • contours above 0 (solid)
  • contours below 0 (dashed)
  • houses (dots, fitted map)

Contours at ±0.1, ±0.2 and +0.3 on the log-price scale (+0.1 is about 10% above an average location). Both maps share one colour scale. With too few df the hot spots blur into a gentle tilt; with too many for the sample size, the fitted map grows spots that aren't there.
