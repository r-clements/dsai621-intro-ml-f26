Bagging: Averaging Trees Smooths the Staircase

The data are Example B from the notes: n = 100 points with x uniform on [0, 1] and y = f(x) + noise, where f(x) = sin(12(x + 0.2)) / (x + 0.2) and the noise has variance 1. A fully grown regression tree fits a staircase that chases the noise. Bagging grows a fully grown tree on each of B bootstrap samples (n points drawn with replacement) and averages their predictions. Move the slider to average more trees. The top plot shows one training set: watch the staircase's jumps get smaller and the fit get smoother. The bottom plots repeat the whole thing on 200 training sets and show 30 of them: watch the band of fits narrow (the variance roughly halves) while their average stays almost exactly on f (bias rises only slightly). With a single predictor there is nothing for a random forest to hide at a split, so here a random forest and bagging are the same thing. (ISL 8.2.1–8.2.2; ESL 8.7, 15.2.)

Keyboard and screen reader users: Tab to the slider and use the arrow keys to step through B = 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100 and 200 trees (Home and End jump to 1 and 200). "Next training set" shows another of the 200 training sets in the top plot. The squared bias and variance are announced in the statistics line below the slider, they are also in the table at the bottom, and each plot's description gives its numbers.

FitSquared biasVariance Expected test MSE
From 200 simulated training sets. Expected test MSE = 1 (the noise variance) + squared bias + variance. The squared bias is the squared gap between the average fit and f, minus the part of that gap due to averaging only 200 fits (the variance divided by 200); without that correction, a fully grown tree's tiny bias would be swamped by simulation noise.
