1.4 Quick notes

  • x,x2,x3,…,(x−ξ1)+3,…: correlated, numerically unstable. Solutions:
    • standardize x
    • use orthonormal columns (QR decomposition)
    • B-spline basis
  • Choosing K: use C.V.
  • Knot placement: by default, use quantiles

2. Smoothing splines

2.1 The criterion

g^λ=arg⁡ming∑i=1n(yi−g(xi))2+λ∫(g″(t))2dt
Long description of Figure 11

Axes y and x, with nine data points trending upward. A solid curve passes through every point, swinging up and down between them; an arrow from the label “larger ∫(g″(t))2dt” points at it. A dashed curve rises smoothly through the middle of the points without chasing each one.

  • λ=0 ⇒ as wiggly as we want
  • λ→∞ ⇒ OLS line

Written beside these: Bias? Variance?

2.2 Solution

Given (ESL Ex. 5.7): For λ>0 and n≥2 distinct xi, the g that minimizes the criterion is a natural cubic spline with knots at all x1,…,xn.

Transcription note In the original, “natural” is underlined in a second colour and “all” is double-underlined.

Recall: For x1<x2<⋯<xn, x1 and xn are the boundary knots.

3.1 Fitting

Transcription note The original numbers this section 3.1, although it continues Section 2.

g(x)=β0+∑j=1n−1βjbj(x)
B=(|||1x⋯bn−1|||)n×n

Annotation Example (columns 1, x, b2, b3):

B=( 1000 11130 128312 1383 )4×4

Commentary These b2 and b3 are ESL’s natural-spline basis functions N3 and N4 (ESL 5.2.1) for knots at x=0,1,2,3; the entries check out.

The loss:

∑i=1n(yi−g(xi))2=‖y−Bβ‖22=(y−Bβ)T(y−Bβ)

Penalty:

∫(g″(t))2dt=∫(∑j=1n−1βjbj″(t))2dt =∫∑j∑kβjβkbj″(t)bk″(t)dt =∑j∑kβjβk∫bj″(t)bk″(t)dt =βTΩβ

Ωn×n: symmetric, positive semi-definite (eigenvalues ≥0).

Worked beside the penalty:

(β1b1+β2b2)2=(β1b1)2+2β1b1β2b2+(β2b2)2

Commentary The intercept and b1(x)=x have zero second derivative, so their rows and columns of Ω are zero: the penalty never touches the straight-line part. That is why λ→∞ leaves the OLS line.

RSSλ(β)=‖y−Bβ‖22+λβTΩβ=(y−Bβ)T(y−Bβ)+λβTΩβ

Transcription note In the original, the second form is squeezed in above the first, and the square on the norm is written high, where it runs into that line.

⇒β^=(BTB+λΩ)−1BTy

Note: BTB is positive definite (eigenvalues >0).

Looks like ridge regression.

g^λ=Bβ^=B(BTB+λΩ)−1BT⏟Sλy =Sλylinear function of y

Annotation Sλ: linear smoother matrix.

Check it: if λ=0,

S0=B(BTB)−1BT=BB−1(BT)−1BT=I

Annotation An arrow points to the step that splits the inverse: B is square, invertible.

Commentary So with λ=0 the fit interpolates every point. As with H in DSAI 601, the effective degrees of freedom are tr(Sλ), which fall from n at λ=0 toward 2 as λ→∞ (ISL 7.5.2).
