Example: does the dough rise?

  • Y=1: dough rose
  • Y=2: dough didn’t rise
  • X: proofing time (centered)
P(Y=1)=.5:π^1 P(Y=2)=.5:π^2
X|Y=1∼N(0,1) X|Y=2∼N(0,4)
Long description of Figure 1

A horizontal x axis with 0 marked in the middle. Two bell curves are both centred at 0. The curve for Y=1 (variance 1) is tall and narrow; the curve for Y=2 (variance 4) is low and wide, and lies above the narrow one in both tails. The curves cross at about −1.36 and 1.36; the interval between the crossings is marked with a bracket on the axis.

What values of x will we predict Y=1?

δ1(x)=log (12×12πexp(−x22)) =log12−12log2π−x22
δ2(x)=log (12×12π2exp(−x28)) =log12−12log2π−log2−x28

Annotation In both lines, the terms log12 and −12log2π are crossed out: they are the same for both classes, so they cancel when we compare δ1 and δ2.

Written beside the comparison: πkfk(x), the quantity whose log is δk(x).

δ1(x)>δ2(x) −x22>−log2−x28 ⋮ ⇒|x|<1.36

Commentary The classes have the same mean, so LDA, with its shared variance, can’t separate them. QDA can, because the variances differ: the boundary is the pair of points x=±1.36 where the curves in Figure 1 cross. The skipped step is 38x2<log2,x2<1.85.

1. Polynomial regression

1.1 Model

yi=β0+β1xi+β2xi2+⋯+βdxid+εi d.f.=d+1(# of coefficients)

Annotation d.f.: # of knobs the fit can turn to specify the curve. Higher means more flexible.

1.2 Distribution of y^

y^∼N(Bβ,σ2H) H=B(BTB)−1BT
Var(f^(xi))=σ2hii

Sum this over the entire training set:

∑i=1nVar(f^(xi))=σ2tr(H)=σ2(d+1)=σ2d.f.

Annotation Every choice of basis gives the same total variance for a fixed d.f., but changes where the variance is.

In general, a degree-d spline with knots ξ1,…,ξK has

d.f.=(K+1)(d+1)−Kd=K+d+1

Annotation Arrows label the parts of this formula:

  • (K+1)(d+1): # of coefficients, i.e. the total d.f. without constraints, from K+1 polynomials (Figure 7).
  • Kd: d constraints per knot.
  • Cubic: d=3, d.f. =K+4.

2.2 Cubic splines (d.f. K+4)

yi=β0+β1b1(xi)+β2b2(xi)+β3b3(xi)+⋯+βK+3bK+3(xi)+εi

Example. 3 knots gives us β4,β5,β6. What are b4,b5,b6?

Long description of Figure 8

Axes y and x, with knots marked at x=3 (dashed line) and x=6 (solid line). The x axis is highlighted in two parts. The part left of 3 is labelled h(x,3)=0. The part right of 3 is labelled h(x,3)=(x−3)3.

Transcription note In the original, the highlight right of 3 is drawn in two shades, one from 3 to about 6 and one from about 6 onward. The labels only distinguish left of 3 from right of 3.

And for the knot at 6:

h(x,6)= { (x−6)3x≥6 0x<6

Commentary So b1,b2,b3 are x,x2,x3, and each knot adds one more: b3+k(x)=h(x,ξk) (ISL 7.4.3). The > on one line and ≥ on the other don’t matter, since (x−ξ)3 is 0 at the knot.
